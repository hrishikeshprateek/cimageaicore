# CIMAGE editorial style guide (derived from cimage.in/blog, September 2026)

Voice
- First-person institutional voice: "we", "our campus", "the CIMAGE family". The institution is "CIMAGE" or "CIMAGE Group of Institutions" (never "the college" alone on first mention).
- Formal but warm and celebratory; proud of Bihar and Patna. Aspirational closing lines about students' futures.
- English. Hindi quotes may be kept in Devanagari with an English rendering right after, in italics.

Structure (typical post ≈ 900–1,300 words)
- Headline: specific and newsy, often "<Event/Topic> at CIMAGE: <what it means>" or a striking quote as the headline.
- Opening: one or two sentences that set the stakes or ask a question, then who/what/where/when in the first paragraph.
- 4–7 H2 subheadings that read like narrative beats ("A Campus Tour That Turned Into an Endorsement", "Day 1: …", "Key Takeaways from …").
- Bullet lists for learning objectives, highlights, key takeaways; no numbered lists in the body.
- Close with a forward-looking paragraph and a call to action: visit the Admissions page or the college office.

People
- Honorifics as the website uses them: "Honourable Chief Minister, Shri Samrat Choudhary", "Prof. (Dr.) Neeraj Agrawal, Director", "Director Megha Agrawal".
- Students, trainers and guests who are not named in the source material are described generically ("participants", "students", "the trainer") - never invent names.

Quotes
- Only verbatim quotes from the source material, attributed, on their own line, italicised.

SEO
- Mention "Patna" and "Bihar" naturally in the opening, at least one subheading and the close.
- Meta description ≤ 155 characters; 6–12 tags mixing topic + location ("AI workshop in Patna", "CIMAGE Patna").
- Recurring facts that may be used only when relevant: motto "Knowledge, Skill, Success"; Wipro Centre of Excellence; Google partnership; IIT Bombay remote centre; programmes BBA, BCA, MBA/PGDM, MCA, BSc IT, B.Com (P).

Never
- Never state a date, number, name or outcome that is not in the evidence. If the evidence is thin, say less.
